Component 2 · Today · concepts

Capture is one stream. So what is Today?

In the locked capture surface a note and a task are typed the same way, into the same document. That decision quietly breaks the usual answer to “what goes in Today”, because the stream isn’t a task list you can filter — it’s a record of what was on your mind.

Four different answers below, each one built far enough to click. They are not variations: they disagree with each other about what the surface is for.

aToday = tasks dated today. The obvious answer, and probably too thin.
bToday = something you choose, not something computed for you.
cNotes are the reason tasks exist. How does that context show up without a feed?
01

The Three

intention · hard cap

Today is a promise you make in the morning, not a query result. Three slots, empty at 7am. Nothing arrives in them on its own — every item is there because you put it there. Everything else in the stream still exists; it is simply not today’s problem.

Pick from the stream
Tap an empty slot to fill it · picking a note promotes it into a task and keeps the note as its reason · tick all three to see the mid-morning state

Good at

  • It makes the hard choice visible. A filtered list lets you avoid deciding; three slots make the decision the interaction.
  • Completion is real. The list can actually end — which no date-filtered Today ever does.
  • Notes get a purpose. Promotion from the stream is the natural way to fill a slot, so notes stop being a graveyard.
  • Nothing is deleted. Unpicked items sit under one collapsed line with no colour, no badge and no count of shame.

What it costs

  • Three is a lie on some days. A day of eleven small errands doesn’t fit the model, and forcing it feels precious.
  • Deadlines don’t care. If you don’t pick the thing that’s due, nothing catches it — the surface needs a quiet, non-nagging line for dated items you skipped.
  • Requires a ritual. If you never do the morning pick, you land on three empty slots and the app is useless.

Finishing all three at 10:40

The mistake would be to auto-refill. The screen instead states plainly that the commitment is met, and offers exactly one more slot — never another three — beside an equally weighted “or stop”. Adding a fourth is a second, deliberate promise, not the list quietly regenerating itself.

02

Today’s Page

document · no list at all

Today isn’t a view of your data — Today is a note, dated today, and it is the same editor as capture. You don’t “add to today”, you write today. Tasks are lines in that page; yesterday’s unfinished lines appear at the top, greyed, and you choose to keep or drop each one.

page
↵ new line · type []  or -  to change a line
Actually type in it · [] makes a task, - makes a bullet · × drops a carried line

Good at

  • Zero new concepts. If capture is a document, this is capture with today’s date on it — one mental model for the whole product.
  • Context is free. The note explaining the task is literally the line above it. No linking feature required.
  • Honest carry-over. Yesterday’s leftovers don’t silently roll forward; you re-type or dismiss them, which is a real review.
  • Writing prose next to your tasks is where thinking actually happens — this is the only concept that allows it.

What it costs

  • No overview. Something due today that you didn’t write onto the page is invisible. That is a correctness bug for anyone with deadlines.
  • It rots. By 4pm the page is a scroll of half-thoughts and struck-through lines; there is no structure to lean on.
  • Bad on a phone in a queue. Free-form editing is a laptop posture; the other three work one-thumbed.
  • Retrieval moves to search. “What am I meant to do?” becomes a query, not a glance.

Where it belongs

Not as the Today surface, but this is almost certainly the right shape for the day’s journal underneath one — and it’s the cheapest concept to build, because it’s the capture editor with a date filter.

03

Because

filter · with provenance

Keep the obvious answer — Today is what’s dated today — but fix the thing that makes it thin: a bare task line has lost its reason. Every task remembers the note it came out of, and one tap unfolds that note in place. Related notes you never promoted sit at the bottom, one tap from becoming work.

context stays folded until you ask for it
Tap from a note under a task to unfold why it exists · → today promotes a note into the list

Good at

  • It answers the actual morning question, which is rarely “what” and almost always “wait, why did I write this”.
  • Calm by default. Context is one line of monospace until requested — it can’t become a feed, because nothing expands on its own.
  • Promotion has a home. Notes surface exactly where a decision about them is cheap, instead of in a separate inbox you never open.
  • Least disruptive. It’s the standard Today everyone already knows, so it needs no ritual and no teaching.

What it costs

  • Still computed. You never decide what today is; the date field does, and an eleven-item Today is as demoralising as ever.
  • Depends on links existing. If the promotion path isn’t how tasks are usually born, most rows have no “why” and the idea evaporates.
  • Relevance is a guess. Choosing which unpromoted notes to show is a ranking problem, and a wrong guess is clutter.
  • The stream doesn’t get smaller. It just gets better annotated.

The real contribution

“Why this task exists” is a mechanic, not a concept. It costs one monospace line, it makes the notes-and-todos-are-one-object bet legible, and it can be dropped into any of the other three.

04

Room

capacity · shaped by time

The thing that ruins Today isn’t which items — it’s how many. So Today is a container with visible walls: three parts of a day, each with room for a couple of things. You don’t pick three; you place everything, and the surface tells you honestly when a stretch is over-subscribed instead of pretending eleven tasks fit.

notes cost nothing — they never take a slot
Tap any task to move it between parts of the day, or out of today entirely · the amber rule marks what doesn’t fit

Good at

  • It argues with you. The only concept that tells you the plan is impossible before the day proves it at 6pm.
  • Triage is continuous, not a morning ceremony — so it survives the days you skip the ritual.
  • Overflow is visible, not blocked. You can over-subscribe; you just can’t do it unknowingly.
  • Maps onto how days actually break — before lunch, after lunch, evening — without touching a calendar.

What it costs

  • Capacity is fiction. “Room for two” assumes tasks are comparable; renewing a domain and rewriting onboarding are not the same object.
  • Most work per item. Every task needs placing, so a busy stream turns into an admin session.
  • Notes are second class here by design — they don’t consume capacity, so they sit in a strip at the top and are easy to ignore.
  • Ambient guilt: an over-subscribed band is a permanent amber mark on your day.

Where it belongs

Phase two. The capacity idea is genuinely good, but it only earns its keep once there’s enough history to make the numbers real rather than a setting you invented.

Recommendation

Ship 01, with 03’s provenance line built into it.

The Three is the only concept that changes the product’s promise. Everything else on the market — Things, Todoist, TickTick, Reminders — computes Today from a date field, so competing there means competing on filters. A surface where the list is a commitment you made, can be finished, and never refills itself is a different product, and it’s the one that fits a capture stream you’re never going to fully process.

On its own it has one real hole: a hard cap of three ignores things that are actually due. That’s a line item, not a redesign — a single quiet monospace row under the three (“2 dated today you didn’t pick”), no red, tappable, and the hole is closed without the surface turning back into a filter.

From 03, take the “from a note” line and put it under any picked item that came out of the stream. It is the cheapest possible expression of the notes-and-todos-are-the-same-object bet — one line of monospace, folded by default, unfolding the note that caused the task. That single detail is what makes Today feel like it belongs to this app rather than to any other list.

Hold 02 as the day’s journal beneath Today rather than as Today itself — it’s the capture editor with a date, so it costs almost nothing later — and hold 04 until real completion history exists, because invented capacity numbers are worse than none.
